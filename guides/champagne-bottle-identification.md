# Champagne Bottle Identification and Release Verification

Exact product identification came up repeatedly when comparing Dom Pérignon, Krug, Cristal, gift-box versions, and merchant listings.

## Record the exact release

The important fields were:

- producer;
- cuvée;
- vintage;
- edition where applicable;
- release tier, such as standard vs. late release;
- bottle size;
- gift-box vs. loose bottle when packaging matters.

Examples from prior research include Krug Grande Cuvée by edition and the distinction between standard Cristal, Late Release, and Vinothèque-style releases.

## Edition is not vintage

Krug Grande Cuvée editions should not be treated as generic interchangeable non-vintage bottles.

When comparing prices, identify the edition first.

## Standard vs. late release

A late-release bottle should not be compared directly with the ordinary release only by vintage year.

The release program and additional maturation are part of what is being bought.

## Merchant listings need verification

Prior shopping research repeatedly encountered uncertainty around:

- exact vintage;
- exact edition;
- whether a gift box was included;
- whether a product image matched the actual bottle;
- whether a listing was in stock or pre-arrival.

For an expensive bottle, seller confirmation can be more useful than relying on a reused product image.

## Packaging is a separate variable

Gift boxes mattered in some purchase comparisons, but packaging should be tracked separately from the wine itself.

A boxed and unboxed bottle can be the same wine while having different presentation or collector value.

## Price comparison

Compare like with like:

- same vintage or edition;
- same release tier;
- same bottle size;
- same packaging status where relevant;
- in-stock vs. pre-arrival;
- landed cost including tax and shipping.

## Scope

Do not expand this page into a general disgorgement-code, counterfeit-detection, or bottle-forensics manual unless those topics are separately researched in conversation.
