# Château d'Yquem Research Notes

Château d'Yquem should be researched as both a wine and a mature-bottle acquisition problem. Vintage quality matters, but for older bottles **provenance and condition can dominate small differences in vintage reputation**.

## 1. Bottle format

Half bottles (375 mL) are especially practical for Sauternes because:

- pours are usually smaller than for dry wine;
- the wine is rich and concentrated;
- a 750 mL bottle can be difficult for a small group to finish;
- mature bottles can be expensive enough that a smaller format lowers the commitment to opening one.

### Format caveat

Bottle size can affect maturation. Smaller bottles generally evolve faster than larger formats because the oxygen-to-wine ratio is higher.

Therefore, do not assume a 375 mL bottle at a given age will show exactly like a 750 mL or magnum.

## 2. Mature-vintage buying

For old d'Yquem, evaluate:

- provenance;
- storage history;
- fill level;
- capsule condition;
- seepage;
- cork position;
- color relative to age;
- label condition;
- bottle format;
- merchant or auction-house reputation;
- return / authenticity policy;
- transport history.

The best vintage on paper can still be the worse purchase if the individual bottle is compromised.

## 3. Color

Sauternes naturally darkens with age.

Color should therefore be interpreted **relative to vintage age and known bottle condition**, not by a simple “darker = bad” rule.

However, unusually advanced color combined with low fill, seepage, or poor storage history can be a warning sign.

## 4. Fill level

For mature bottles, fill is an important but non-exclusive condition signal.

Interpret fill together with:
- bottle age;
- evidence of seepage;
- capsule condition;
- storage history;
- format.

A slightly lower fill in a very old bottle may be understandable; an unexpectedly low fill in a younger bottle deserves more scrutiny.

## 5. Vintage research

Separate three questions:

1. **Vintage quality at release** — weather, botrytis development, concentration, balance.
2. **Current maturity** — where the wine is likely to be in its evolution today.
3. **Bottle realization** — whether the individual bottle was stored well enough to express that potential.

For mature wine, question 3 can matter more than a few points of critic-score difference.

## 6. Style dimensions

Useful tasting dimensions for comparing vintages include:

- sweetness;
- acidity;
- botrytis character;
- citrus vs. tropical fruit;
- honey / caramel development;
- spice;
- concentration;
- freshness;
- finish.

The most highly rated vintage is not automatically the best choice for every purpose. Some drinkers may prefer fresher, more lifted vintages over denser and more opulent ones.

## 7. Opening a mature bottle

Older Sauternes benefits from conservative handling.

Before service:
- stand the bottle upright if sediment is suspected;
- avoid unnecessary transport immediately before opening;
- inspect the cork and capsule;
- have an appropriate opener available for fragile corks.

Decanting should be driven by the bottle's condition and aromatic state rather than applied automatically.

## 8. Serving temperature

Serve cool, but not so cold that aroma and texture are suppressed.

Allowing the wine to evolve gradually in the glass is often more useful than chasing a single exact temperature.

## 9. Pairing

d'Yquem can work beyond dessert.

Common structural matches include:
- foie gras;
- blue cheese;
- rich pâté;
- fruit-based desserts;
- dishes with sweet-savory contrast.

The key is matching sweetness, acidity, salt, fat, and intensity rather than defaulting to “dessert wine = dessert only.”

## 10. Buying hierarchy for old bottles

For a mature bottle, a practical priority order is:

1. provenance;
2. bottle condition;
3. trusted seller;
4. exact format;
5. vintage style and reputation;
6. price.

This hierarchy is intentionally different from buying a young current-release wine.

## Bottom line

With old d'Yquem, the purchase decision is not “which vintage scored highest?” It is “which specific bottle has the best combination of provenance, condition, maturity profile, format, and price?”
