# Mature Bottle Provenance

This page distills issues that came up in prior discussions of older Château d'Yquem and mature Champagne.

## Vintage reputation is not enough

For an older bottle, the specific bottle matters as much as the headline vintage.

The recurring checks were:

- provenance and storage history;
- fill level where relevant;
- capsule/cork condition;
- signs of seepage or heat exposure;
- seller reputation;
- whether the price still makes sense after shipping and tax.

## Shipping is part of provenance

Weather-sensitive shipping was repeatedly discussed.

Useful conclusions:

- avoid exposing valuable bottles to hot-weather ground shipping when a hold or temperature-controlled option is available;
- compare the landed cost of faster/cold-pack shipping rather than treating shipping as an afterthought;
- a slightly cheaper bottle can become the worse purchase if safe shipping is much more expensive.

## Half bottles of d'Yquem

375 ml d'Yquem was specifically attractive because the wine is rich and normally poured in smaller quantities.

The format can make an older or expensive bottle easier to open without needing a large group.

## Bottle condition vs. packaging

For drinking, bottle condition and provenance matter more than cosmetic packaging.

Gift boxes can still matter for presentation or collecting, but they should not be confused with wine condition.

## Conclusion

For a mature bottle intended to be drunk, prioritize:

1. trustworthy provenance and condition;
2. the vintage and maturity profile;
3. total landed cost;
4. packaging only if it matters for the intended use.

## Scope

Do not broaden this page into a generic auction or cellar-inspection manual unless those topics are separately researched in conversation.
