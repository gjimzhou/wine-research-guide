# Mature Bottle Provenance and Buying Checklist

For older fine wine, the question is not only “is this a great vintage?” but **is this specific bottle likely to deliver the vintage's potential?**

As bottle age increases, provenance can dominate small differences in critic score or headline vintage reputation.

## 1. Information to request

For mature bottles, ask for as much of the following as the seller can provide:

- ownership history;
- storage history;
- source of acquisition;
- whether the bottle came from a professional cellar, private cellar, auction, or recent import;
- ullage or fill level where relevant;
- capsule condition;
- cork evidence or seepage;
- label condition;
- signs of heat exposure;
- high-resolution bottle photographs;
- whether the bottle has been recently transported;
- merchant return policy for condition problems.

A seller who cannot document every item is not automatically unsafe, but uncertainty should be reflected in price.

## 2. Provenance is not cosmetic condition

A stained or damaged label can be compatible with excellent storage. Conversely, a beautiful label does not prove good temperature history.

Separate:

- **cosmetic condition** — label, capsule appearance, presentation;
- **physical bottle condition** — fill, seepage, cork, sediment;
- **storage provenance** — temperature, ownership, movement, source.

Collectors may pay for cosmetics; drinkers should prioritize the variables that affect the wine.

## 3. Shipping is part of provenance

The bottle can be damaged after purchase by poor logistics.

For valuable mature wine:

- avoid unnecessary exposure to heat or freezing temperatures;
- use weather holds when appropriate;
- minimize uncontrolled warehouse and delivery time;
- consider faster service when weather windows are narrow;
- let a bottle settle after substantial transport before opening, especially when sediment is present.

## 4. Auction vs. specialist retail

### Auction

Potential advantages:

- access to rare vintages and formats;
- cellar collections;
- price discovery.

Potential disadvantages:

- buyer's premium;
- limited returns;
- provenance uncertainty;
- mixed condition within a lot.

### Specialist retailer

Potential advantages:

- clearer bottle-level condition descriptions;
- easier customer service;
- shipping coordination;
- possible return or credit options.

Potential disadvantages:

- higher headline price;
- retail margin;
- mature inventory may be shallow.

Compare total landed cost and risk-adjusted value, not hammer price vs. retail sticker.

## 5. Half bottles and other formats

Bottle size changes ageing rate, scarcity, and price per unit of wine. For rich sweet wine, half bottles can be practically attractive because a smaller serving format makes it easier to open mature wine without needing a large group.

Do not assume the 375 ml market tracks the 750 ml market proportionally.

## 6. Decision rule

For an old bottle intended to be drunk rather than displayed:

**pay first for trustworthy provenance, then for vintage, then for cosmetics.**

That ordering is not universal, but it is a useful default for consumption-focused buying.
