# Storage and Service

## Temperature

For long-term Champagne and fine-wine storage, stable cellar-like temperature matters more than chasing a perfectly constant single number. Avoid sustained heat and large repeated swings.

## Light

Direct sunlight and strong UV exposure should be minimized. Bottle glass is not a substitute for appropriate placement, window treatment, or enclosed storage.

## Cabinet organization

Useful principles:

- place frequently accessed bottles where they can be removed without disturbing the rest of the cellar;
- reserve larger-clearance shelves for oversized prestige-Champagne bottles and gift boxes;
- keep high-value bottles in stable positions away from vibration and frequent movement;
- account for the fact that real bottle capacity can be far below a cabinet's advertised Bordeaux-bottle count.

## Humidity

Reasonable humidity is useful for cork storage, but in a normal home wine cabinet, temperature stability, darkness, and avoiding vibration are generally more important than micromanaging a precise humidity target.

## Service

Serving temperature should be chosen for the wine, not simply "as cold as possible." Very cold service can suppress aroma and texture, especially for mature or vinous Champagne. A bottle can be served cool and allowed to evolve in the glass.
